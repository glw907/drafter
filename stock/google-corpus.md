# The Google corpus

The drafting seat reads these excerpts for the voice of the
[Google developer documentation style guide](https://developers.google.com/style): short
sentences, the reader addressed directly, and the instruction stated plainly. Imitate the voice
only. Never take an excerpt's facts, terms, product names, or wording into a page.

The excerpts are by Google, from its developer documentation style guide. Each is a few sentences
from one page of the guide, quoted with its title, URL, license, and retrieval date. Each
excerpt's page states its license in its footer, and this file copies nothing from the guide
beyond these excerpts. The excerpts drop the source's italics and code formatting, and change
nothing else.

## Active voice

URL: <https://developers.google.com/style/voice>

License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (<https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/>)

Retrieved: 2026-10-19

> In passive voice, it's easy to neglect to indicate who or what is performing a particular
> action. In this kind of construction, it's often hard for readers to figure out who's supposed
> to do something (such as the reader, the computer, the server, an end user, or a visitor to a
> web page).

## Second person and first person

URL: <https://developers.google.com/style/person>

License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (<https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/>)

Retrieved: 2026-10-19

> In general, address the reader of your documents using the second person instead of the first
> person: use you or your instead of we, our, or us. Assume that the reader is the person who's
> doing the tasks or making the decisions.

## Present tense

URL: <https://developers.google.com/style/tense>

License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (<https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/>)

Retrieved: 2026-10-19

> Use present tense for statements that describe general behavior that's not associated with a
> particular time.

## Procedures

URL: <https://developers.google.com/style/procedures>

License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (<https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/>)

Retrieved: 2026-10-19

> In most cases, introduce a procedure with an introductory sentence. This introductory sentence
> should provide context to the reader that isn't part of the section heading.
